# CFR papers — Claude distillation digest

### deep_pdcfr_paper (Deep (Predictive) Discounted CFR (AAAI-26))
- [high] Cumulative-advantage bootstrapping (single-iteration buffer + bootstrap from prev-iter net): You say you already applied 'DCFR+ 1+2 + bootstrap of cumulative ADVANTAGES'. This paper IS the formal recipe behind that — confirms your design is correct and gives the exact loss L(theta_t)=E[(max(R_{t-1},0)*(t-1)^a/((t-1)^a+1) + r_bar - R_t)^2]. Worth double-checking your discount/clip ORDERING matches theirs (they note the clip-then-discount sequence is adjusted specifically to make sampling-based expectation of r_bar unbiased). A subtle ordering bug here would silently bias your advantages.
- [med] Corrected sampled counterfactual value (drop opponent reach, target the ADVANTAGE not the regret): You use external-sampling MCCFR (not outcome sampling), so the exact importance-weighting math differs — but the PRINCIPLE applies: make your net regress a quantity whose target magnitude is stable across infosets (advantages, not opponent-reach-weighted CFVs). Confirm your ES advantage targets aren't carrying a residual reach-probability scale that the net must fight across magnitudes. Low-effort sanity check, potentially high payoff for training stability.
- [high] DREAM-style history value network as variance-reduction baseline: This is your single biggest OPEN gap ('NO variance-reduction baseline yet'). This is the directly-applicable component. Caveat: DREAM/this baseline is derived for OUTCOME sampling (single action sampled, hence the 1/xi correction). You run EXTERNAL sampling, where the updating player already explores all actions and variance comes only from opponent/chance sampling — so the control-variate form must be re-derived for ES (baseline applied at chance/opponent nodes), and the variance win is smaller than in OS. Still worth it, but don't copy the OS formula verbatim. Expect modest, not dramatic, reduction of your +/-8 bb/100 noise.
- [med] Direct strategy as sampled strategy (avoid IS in average-strategy buffer): You maintain a separate time-averaged policy net. Under ES your averaging weights differ from OS, but the takeaway — store clean sigma_t plus an explicit iteration/reach weight rather than IS-scaled strategies — could stabilize your policy-net training. Minor; your policy net isn't flagged as the bottleneck.
- [med] DCFR+/PDCFR+ averaging discount (t/T)^gamma in the average-strategy loss: You report 'gamma=2 averaging' already. This confirms the exact loss-weighting form. If your policy net currently weights samples by t^gamma rather than (t/T)^gamma, the normalization-by-T detail keeps targets bounded — a tiny but worthwhile alignment.
- [low] PDCFR+ predictive extension (instantaneous-advantage net to predict next-iter cumulative advantage): Optional next step AFTER variance reduction. Notably, in the paper's actual POKER experiment (FHP) PDCFR+ did NOT beat DCFR+ (11.3 vs 11.6, within noise) — the predictive trick mainly helps non-poker IIGs (Battleship, Goofspiel, Liar's Dice). So for your HUNL target, adding the third net is low expected value vs cost. Defer/skip.
- [med] Empirical evidence on FHP (Flop Hold'em Poker, a real poker game): Most relevant data point: it's the only poker result and it confirms the combo you're building (DCFR+ bootstrap + VR baseline) is the right direction for poker specifically. BUT: their eval is vs hand-crafted rule-based bots, NOT exploitability and NOT vs a strong GTO baseline like GTO Wizard — so it does NOT predict your bb/100 vs GTO Wizard AI. Treats your -72 bb/100 problem (broad postflop quality) as orthogonal; this paper accelerates CONVERGENCE, it does nothing for your crude fcpa abstraction or hand-crafted features, which are the more likely root causes of postflop weakness.
- [med] Bootstrap-not-retrain training efficiency claim: Directly relevant given your bottleneck is the ~1.9s/iter Python traverse (CPU), not the net. Bootstrapping means smaller per-iter buffers and fewer SGD steps — helps wall-clock if you warm-start the advantage net from prev-iter weights instead of re-initializing. Won't fix the traverse bottleneck, but reduces GPU-side cost so you can spend more on traversals.
- [low] ESCHER comparison / rejection rationale: Useful design-decision context: if you later want to kill importance sampling entirely you'd look at ESCHER, but the paper's experience says it's expensive/finicky. For now stick with the DREAM baseline route. Informational only.
- [med] Network architecture / hyperparameters used: Mostly a sanity reference. Two concrete takeaways: (1) normalize utilities to [-1,1] before the net (your chip frame is 20000-scale — confirm you scale targets, else advantages span huge magnitudes the paper explicitly warns nets handle poorly); (2) your alpha=2/gamma=2 matches their DCFR+ exactly. Their tiny 64-128 nets are for smaller games — don't read net SIZE guidance into your HUNL build.

### seq_equilibrium_cfr_paper (CFR for Sequential Equilibrium (ICLR-26))
- [low] Sequential equilibrium refinement via decreasing local perturbation (SPCFR): Conceptually attractive for our stated goal: beating GTO Wizard AI is a NE-vs-NE benchmark, but exploiting humans/weak bots happens in off-path infosets where pure NE is arbitrary. SPCFR would make those branches non-arbitrary. BUT this is mostly orthogonal to our -72 bb/100 problem, which is broad postflop QUALITY on the equilibrium path, not weird off-path play. Refinement won't move AIVAT vs a near-optimal opponent much. Treat as a later polish, not a fix.
- [med] Perturbed regret matching mixed with uniform: sigma_T = (1-delta_T)*beta_T + delta_T/|A|: Tabular formulation; in Deep CFR the strategy comes from a regret/advantage net, not explicit regret matching per infoset, so the exact mechanism doesn't drop in. The transferable idea is forced exploration: blending a small uniform floor into the sampled traversal policy could improve coverage of rarely-visited postflop infosets, which our 80-traversal CPU-bottlenecked sampler under-explores. That MIGHT marginally help our broad-quality problem. Low-effort experiment, uncertain payoff.
- [low] Local vs global perturbation argument (Theorem 2): HUNL is deep (4 streets, capped raises), so the depth-decay warning is directly relevant IF we ever add perturbation. It tells us not to use a naive global exploration epsilon and instead scale exploration per-infoset by visit count. Useful design caveat but again only matters once we decide to pursue refinement/exploration at all.
- [low] Adding perturbation to DCFR rather than CFR+ (their experimental choice): We already run DCFR+ (alpha=2, clip, bootstrap, gamma=2 averaging) with Leduc neural exploitability dropping 445->338, so the DCFR base is compatible in principle. Evidence that perturbation does NOT degrade exploitability is reassuring but the paper only validates tabular Kuhn/Leduc/Goofspiel, not neural or HUNL-scale. No demonstrated benefit at our scale.
- [low] Evaluation metric: maximum information-set regret as an EFPE/SE proxy: We already have exact Leduc exploitability as a correctness proof; max-infoset regret is computable there too and could expose whether our DCFR+ leaves bad off-path actions. Cheap diagnostic on Leduc, but does not transfer to neural HUNL (no tabular regrets) and is not our bottleneck.
- [low] Claim that NE plays suboptimally at unreachable infosets vs irrational opponents: Diagnostically useful framing but does NOT explain our -72 bb/100. GTO Wizard AI is near-optimal, so the relevant infosets ARE on-path; our loss is on-path postflop quality. This paper addresses a different failure mode (exploiting weak opponents) than the one we are measuring. Honest verdict: not a lever for the current benchmark.

### weva_abstraction_paper (WEVA (arXiv 2605.10900, Tsinghua 2026))
- [low] Core premise: WEVA replaces hand-crafted info abstraction with k-means on warm-up CFR per-hand EVs: Architecturally orthogonal to our build. We use a NEURAL Deep CFR net that learns over raw features end-to-end — there is no explicit bucket mapping to replace. Our 20-dim strength-bucketed feature is a *feature representation*, not a CFR info-abstraction in WEVA's sense. WEVA cannot be dropped into deep_cfr_hunl.py as-is. Conceptually interesting only as motivation to reduce reliance on hand-crafted equity buckets.
- [low] Key empirical claim: EV rank-ordering stabilizes after ~W=10 CFR iterations (Spearman ρ>0.996) long before strategy converges: Mildly relevant intuition: early-iteration advantage-net EV estimates already carry most of the *ordinal* hand-strength signal even before our nets converge. Could justify using cheap early-iter EV signals to seed/augment features. But our broad -72 bb/100 postflop loss is a strategy-quality problem, not a bucket-ordering problem, so this insight does not address our actual gap.
- [med] ev-nd: depth-weighted multi-node EV feature vector vs single root EV (equity-like): This is the one transferable idea, but only as a feature-engineering hint. Our 20-dim feature collapses hand strength into a scalar treys-normalized bucket plus crude hole-card flags — it is structurally an 'equity/rank' style feature and will miss blocker/board-interaction effects the paper shows matter. We could ADD per-node or per-action conditional EV-style features (or richer board-interaction features) to give the net signal beyond scalar strength. However the paper provides these via expensive tabular full-game CFR, which is infeasible for full HUNL (they themselves note WEVA can't be applied to full HUNL).
- [low] Scope limitation explicitly stated: WEVA requires the full unabstracted game to fit in memory; cannot be applied to full HUNL: Decisive disqualifier for direct use. Our target is full multi-street HUNL solved by neural self-play precisely BECAUSE tabular full-game CFR is intractable. WEVA's mechanism is the thing our entire architecture exists to avoid. Confirms this paper is not a path to fixing our floor.
- [low] Suggested future work: WEVA as online abstraction inside belief-based depth-limited subgame resolving (DeepStack/Supremus lineage): Only relevant to a FUTURE version of our bot that adds real-time resolving + CFV value nets (a known SOTA gap). Until we have a resolving stack (which we explicitly do not — bot samples policy net directly), there is nothing to plug WEVA into. Note for the roadmap, not actionable now.
- [low] DCFR hyperparameters used as solver baseline (α=1.5, β=0, γ=2): Tangential corroboration of our DCFR+ direction (we use α=2, γ=2). Their α=1.5,β=0 differs; no strong reason to change ours since our Leduc exploitability is already improving under our settings. Could try β=0 ablation, but low priority vs the bottlenecks (no variance reduction, crude betting, slow Python traverse).
- [low] Validation methodology: exact exploitability on small games + Spearman ρ vs near-oracle EV from 50k iters: Marginally reinforces our existing Leduc exact-exploitability validation harness. The Spearman-vs-oracle diagnostic could be a cheap sanity check: compare our advantage-net's per-hand EV ranking against a long tabular Leduc solve to detect if the net learns correct hand ordering early. A minor optional diagnostic, not a fix.

### nn_architecture_paper (Optimized NN architecture for classification (Nadeem 2026))
- [low] MLP with 2-3 hidden layers, 64-128 neurons, ReLU: Our advantage/policy nets are also small MLPs over a 20-dim hand-crafted feature vector, so a 2-3 layer / 64-256 unit ReLU body is a sane default sizing reference. But this is common knowledge already standard in Deep CFR implementations; the paper adds nothing specific (no residuals, no normalization tricks that matter at our scale). The -72 bb/100 floor is from abstraction/features/no-resolving, not from MLP width.
- [low] Adam optimizer + lr=0.001: We already train the regression nets with a gradient optimizer; Adam at ~1e-3 is the conventional starting point and we likely use it. No new information — and DCFR weighting/loss matters far more for our exploitability than the optimizer choice.
- [low] Dropout + L2 regularization for generalization: Mild relevance: Deep CFR advantage nets are trained on noisy, non-stationary regression targets from a replay buffer and CAN overfit late-iteration samples. A small amount of L2 / dropout could marginally stabilize the advantage net. However, in CFR the standard remedy is reservoir sampling + retraining from scratch each iteration, not classification-style dropout; over-regularizing a regression target can hurt. Worth a quick ablation at best.
- [low] Min-Max feature normalization / scaling: We already use ratio-based and bucketed features (pot/owe/remaining ratios, treys-normalized strength) that are roughly bounded. Confirming all 20 features are scaled to a consistent range is good hygiene, but we appear to already do this (verified 0 feature-reconstruction mismatches). No new technique.
- [low] Classification metrics (accuracy/precision/recall/F1/AUC) + softmax + cross-entropy: Not applicable. Our core nets do regression (advantage values) and a strategy distribution trained toward time-averaged regret-matching, evaluated by exploitability (Leduc) and AIVAT bb/100 (HUNL) — not by accuracy/F1/AUC. The paper's metric and loss apparatus does not transfer to a CFR value net.
- [low] NAS / evolutionary architecture search (literature survey only): Not actionable: NAS is wildly over-engineered for a 20-input MLP and is only cited, not implemented, in this paper. Our bottleneck is the Python traverse (1.9 s/iter) and the abstraction/feature ceiling, not architecture micro-optimization. Skip.
- [low] Overall framework (UCI-tabular classification pipeline): Fundamentally mismatched to a self-play Deep CFR setting. There is no fixed labeled dataset, no train/val/test split, no static distribution — targets are generated on-the-fly by MCCFR traversal and shift every iteration. None of the paper's pipeline, datasets, or claimed 97% accuracy results inform a from-scratch HUNL CFV/strategy net. Honest verdict: this paper is generic and not relevant to our build.
